# Habit tracker competitor research

Date: 2026-11-15

The market is crowded but its leaders share the same weaknesses: punishing streaks, paywalled basics and lost data.

**Source quality:** mostly blog roundups and review summaries, several written by competing apps. Treat numbers as approximate. Reddit threads could not be reached directly.

## Main competitors

| App | Position | Pricing (approx.) | Praised for | Criticised for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Streaks | Minimal, Apple-only | ~$5 one-time | Design, no subscription, Apple Health auto-logging, local data | 12-habit cap, no Android, streaks broken by Health or iCloud gaps |
| Loop | Free, open-source, Android | Free, no ads | Privacy, charts, CSV export | Android only, plain look |
| Habitica | RPG gamification | Free core, ~$5/month | Free core tracking, social parties | Heavy setup before first habit, fatigue after a few weeks, inactive party members hurt you |
| Habitify | Cross-platform analytics | ~$30–35/year or ~$65 lifetime | Data and trends, works everywhere | Only 3 free habits |
| Finch | Self-care virtual pet | Sources conflict: $30–70/year | Pet companionship, gentle tone, ~4.9 from hundreds of thousands of ratings | Subscription cost, bugs, slow support |
| Productive | Routine blocks | ~$7/month or $30/year | Clean visuals, morning/evening routines | Sync, reminders and watch features paywalled |
| Atoms | Identity-based (Atomic Habits) | ~$10/month (single source) | Beginner-friendly coaching | Price |
| Way of Life | Long-running, colour calendar | ~$5 one-time | Solid data model | Dated UI, 3 free habits |

### Plant and garden-themed apps

Garden's closest neighbours are small: Habit Garden, Build & Break, Gentle: Habit Garden, HabitLeaf and Habit Plant. None appears to have meaningful review volume yet.

They split on what a missed day does:

- Plants wither (Build & Break).
- Growth pauses (Habit Garden).
- Rest days don't count against you (Gentle, pitched at ADHD users).

## What users praise

- **One-time pricing or a genuinely free core:** Streaks, Loop and Habitica's free tier get credit for this.
- **Low-friction logging:** widgets, watch apps and automatic logging from Apple Health.
- **Emotional attachment:** Finch's pet is the standout example.
- **Privacy:** local data with no tracking or account.

## What they lack (recurring complaints)

- **Streak anxiety:** people describe losing "an 87-day streak" rather than missing a habit, and some log habits they didn't do to protect the number.
- **Data and streak loss:** reinstalls, phone changes, sync errors and OS updates wipe history.
- **Paywalled basics:** three-habit free caps, and widgets or sync moved behind subscriptions after the fact.
- **Gamification fatigue:** points, levels and pets wear off around weeks four to six, according to one review analysis.
- **Unreliable reminders:** either spammy or silently stopped by battery optimisation.
- **Retention overall:** a 2024 JMIR study pooled health apps and found about 70% of users stop within 100 days.

## Most requested features

1. Streak freezes, skip days or rest days that don't reset progress.
2. Flexible schedules: X times per week, specific weekdays, multiple completions per day.
3. Automatic tracking from Health data and other apps.
4. Home and lock screen widgets that show progress and allow logging.
5. Reliable backup and cross-device sync, plus CSV export.
6. Negative-habit tracking (things to avoid).
7. No subscription, or a one-time unlock.

In one Hacker News thread of 10 commenters, automatic logging (5) and privacy (4) came up most.

## What this suggests for Garden

- The clearest gap is a forgiving tracker with emotional pull that doesn't punish a missed day.
- A flower that wilts hard would recreate the streak anxiety people complain about; the "watered today looks hopeful" direction avoids that.
- Reliable backup and a generous free tier are the other two places incumbents are visibly weak.
